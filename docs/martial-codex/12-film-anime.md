# Film & Anime Fighting Styles, Grounded in Reality

The best screen fighting is not invented — it is **real martial arts, choreographed for the camera**. This section maps the most influential film and anime styles back to the living arts they were built from, names the choreographers and consultants who did it, and rates how grounded each one actually is (`realism` 0–10). It is the sister of the [video-game section](11-games-cqc.md): together they feed Section 3's *Fusion Lab*. Provenance tiers: **(A)** creator/choreographer primary statement · **(B)** journalistic/analytic consensus · **(C)** fan attribution · **(D)** speculation/myth.

---

## John Wick — "Gun-Fu"

**On screen:** A seamless blend of pistol/carbine shooting with judo throws, jiu-jitsu transitions, and weapon retention — fired in close, reloaded mid-grapple, opponents flowing from standing to ground.

**Real roots:** The action was built by **87Eleven Action Design**, founded by *John Wick* directors **Chad Stahelski** and **David Leitch** (both ex-stuntmen), with **Jonathan "JoJo" Eusebio** as fight coordinator (B). Eusebio trained at the **Inosanto Academy** (Jeet Kune Do / Filipino arts), and the unarmed vocabulary is a documented mix of **judo and Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu** (B). For the firearms, **Keanu Reeves trained 3-Gun competition shooting at Taran Tactical** (B). The films explicitly homage the "gun-fu" of **John Woo's** Hong Kong cinema (B).

> **Myth-bust (important):** It is widely repeated online that Wick's shooting is the **Center Axis Relock (CAR)** system. CAR's own authorities dispute this — *"that's not really Center Axis Relock in those movies… it wasn't us"* — and analysts note Keanu mostly uses a **modified high-ready position**, not CAR (C/D). We rate the CAR attribution **(D)** and the judo/BJJ/3-Gun core **(B)**.

**Realism:** **7/10.** The individual pieces — judo, BJJ, practical pistol — are real and well-executed; the *volume* and invincibility are pure cinema. **Train to emulate:** judo + BJJ + a reputable defensive-pistol/3-Gun course.

## Jason Bourne — Kali + Jeet Kune Do

**On screen:** Brutal, economical, improvised — a rolled magazine or a book becomes a weapon; fast trapping and limb destructions, no wasted motion.

**Real roots:** Chief fight coordinator **Jeff Imada** — a senior **Dan Inosanto** student and close friend of Brandon Lee — choreographed *The Bourne Supremacy* and *The Bourne Ultimatum* and trained Matt Damon. Imada states the primary styles are **Filipino Kali/Eskrima and Jeet Kune Do** (A/B). The improvised-weapon work (the famous **pen** and **rolled-magazine** fights) is straight FMA logic: *the weapon is an extension of the empty hand.*

**Realism:** **8/10** — among the most grounded mainstream action; little wirework, real movement principles. **Train to emulate:** Filipino Kali (Inosanto blend) + JKD + boxing.

## Bruce Lee — Jeet Kune Do

**On screen:** Explosive speed, the one-inch power, the side kick, the intercepting stop-hit.

**Real roots:** Lee studied **Wing Chun under Ip Man** in Hong Kong, then — after his circa-1964 duel with **Wong Jack-man** — abandoned fixed systems and named his own approach **Jeet Kune Do** ("Way of the Intercepting Fist") in **1967** (A/B). JKD layers Wing Chun, **Western boxing**, and **fencing** footwork/timing (Lee's brother Peter was a champion fencer — the "interception" is a fencing stop-hit) (B). See also the [Chinese Martial Arts section](02-chinese-martial-arts.md).

**Realism:** **8/10** — it *is* a real art that Lee actually practiced; the films simply showcase the best version of it. The conceptual ancestor of modern MMA.

## Ip Man (Donnie Yen) — Wing Chun

**Real roots:** The 2008 *Ip Man* (dir. **Wilson Yip**) depicts the real Wing Chun grandmaster and Bruce Lee's teacher. Action by **Sammo Hung** (with coordinator **Tony Leung Siu-hung**); Wing Chun lineage consultants included **Ip Chun** (Ip Man's son) and, by *Ip Man 3*, **Ip Ching** (B). Donnie Yen dropped ~14 kg to match Ip Man's frame (B). The art's **Ng Mui / Yim Wing-chun** origin legend is **(C)** and disputed.

**Realism:** **6/10** — the trapping, centerline theory, and chain-punching are real Wing Chun, but cinematically accelerated far past sparring speed. **Train to emulate:** Wing Chun (with the honest caveat that it underperforms vs resisting grapplers/kickers — see Section 02).

## Ong-Bak (Tony Jaa) — Muay Boran

**Real roots:** Director **Prachya Pinkaew**; action choreographed by **Panna Rittikrai**, Tony Jaa's real-life mentor. The style is **Muay Boran**, the older battlefield ancestor of [Muay Thai](03-southeast-south-asian.md), and the film was marketed on its authenticity: *"No computer graphics. No stunt doubles. No wires."* (B)

**Realism:** **8/10** — real Muay Boran elbows, knees, and flying techniques performed for real. **Train to emulate:** Muay Thai → Muay Boran.

---

## The Raid (Iko Uwais) — Pencak Silat

**Real roots:** Welsh director **Gareth Evans** came to Indonesia to film a **documentary about Pencak Silat** and discovered **Iko Uwais** (a real **Silat Tiga Berantai / Silat Betawi** practitioner) and **Yayan Ruhian** (**Pencak Silat Tenaga Dasar**). The two **co-choreographed both films** (B). The close-quarters blade-and-empty-hand brutality is genuine [Silat](03-southeast-south-asian.md).

**Realism:** **8/10** — real Silat masters, no wires, blistering pace. **Train to emulate:** Pencak Silat (Harimau / Betawi).

---

## Jackie Chan — Acrobatic Hong Kong / Drunken Boxing

**Real roots:** Chan trained from age ~7 in **Peking opera** at the **China Drama Academy** under **Master Yu Jim-yuen**, becoming one of the **Seven Little Fortunes** (alongside Sammo Hung and Yuen Biao). *Drunken Master* (1978, dir. **Yuen Woo-ping**) and *Drunken Master II* (1994) showcase cinematic **Zui Quan** (drunken boxing) with Chan as a young **Wong Fei-hung** (B). The acrobatics and prop-fighting are real opera-derived skill.

**Realism:** **6/10** — extraordinary real athleticism and timing, but the choreography is comedic and prop-driven, not combat-optimal.

## Avatar: The Last Airbender — Bending as Real Chinese Martial Arts

The strongest reality-built fiction here. Chief martial-arts consultant **Sifu Kisu** of the **Harmonious Fist Chinese Athletic Association** (a 5th-generation Northern Shaolin practitioner) worked on all 61 episodes, mapping each element to a **distinct living CMA style** and motion-capturing real practitioners (A):

| Bending | Real art | Character archetype |
|---|---|---|
| **Water** | **Tai Chi (Yang style)** — flowing, redirecting | Katara, Pakku |
| **Earth** | **Hung Gar** — low rooted stances, direct power | Most earthbenders |
| **Fire** | **Northern Shaolin** — dynamic long-range limbs | Zuko, Azula |
| **Air** | **Baguazhang** — circular evasive circle-walking | Aang |
| **Toph (sub-style)** | **Chu Gar Southern Praying Mantis** — low, close, sensing-based | Toph |

> **Correction (verified):** Toph's distinct style was handled by a **second** consultant, **Sifu Manuel Rodriguez** (a Chu Gar master) — *not* Sifu Kisu — and his son **Joshua Murphy Rodriguez** was Toph's motion model (A, per the *Avatar Spirits* documentary). Conflating "Kisu" with "Manny Rodriguez" is a common error. Also: **chi-blocking** debuted in ATLA (Ty Lee), not Korra; *Korra* later blended CMA with **MMA/UFC and tricking** for pro-bending (consulted MMA fighters Mac Danzig and Jeremy Umphries) (A/B).

**Realism:** **8/10** — the *movements* are faithful, expert CMA; only the elemental powers are fantasy. The single best on-ramp for "I want to learn what I saw on screen."

## Baki the Grappler — Real Arts, Superhuman Execution

Creator **Keisuke Itagaki** boxed during military service and plots fights from experience. The roster is a who's-who of real arts (karate, judo, BJJ/Gracie vale tudo, boxing, Muay Thai, sumo, kung fu) and real-fighter homages: **Doppo Orochi ← Mas Oyama** (Kyokushin), **Biscuit Oliva ← bodybuilder Sergio Oliva**, **Retsu Kaioh ← Bruce Lee**, **"Rei Zulu" ← vale-tudo legend Casemiro Martins**, **"Dickson" ← Rickson Gracie** (B). *(Correction: "Mount Toba" is a **Giant Baba pro-wrestling** homage, not part of the grappling/Oliva thread.)*

**Realism:** **6/10** — the named arts and techniques are real; the physiology and outcomes are deliberately cranked to superhuman/supernatural. Dramatized homage, not simulation.

## Kenichi: The Mightiest Disciple — One Master per Real Style

Each Ryōzanpaku master embodies a discrete real discipline: **Sakaki = Karate**, **Akisame = Jūjutsu/Jūdō**, **Apachai = Muay Thai / Muay Boran**, **Kensei Ma = Chinese kenpō / Taijiquan + weapons**, **Shigure = weapons/ninjutsu** (B). Often cited as one of the more *educational* fight manga because it labels and contrasts schools.

**Realism:** **7/10** — conceptually faithful technique, exaggerated power/speed.

## Hajime no Ippo & Megalo Box — Boxing, Real and Fantastical

**Hajime no Ippo** (George Morikawa, a former **cornerman and boxing journalist**) is the **most technically realistic** fight series in this set: the **Dempsey Roll** (from heavyweight champ **Jack Dempsey**'s book), the **liver blow** (à la **Julio César Chávez**), and the **gazelle punch** (**Floyd Patterson**) are all real, named techniques (B). **Realism: 9/10.**

**Megalo Box** is a **50th-anniversary homage to *Ashita no Joe*** whose boxers wear powered exoskeleton "**Gear**" (the hero, "Gearless Joe," fights without it). Faithful as *human drama and tribute*, fantastical as boxing. **Realism: 4/10** — but it earns a place as the bridge to our *Fusion Lab* sci-fi concept (powered-exosuit combat).

## Kengan Ashura — Researched, then Stylized

Author **Yabako Sandrovich** is a genuine martial artist; fighters use **named** real arts (**Hatsumi-style Aikido**, police **Taihō-jutsu**, **Gōjū-ryū** karate, judo, boxing, sumo). The protagonist's **Niko Style** is a *fictional hybrid built from real arts*, with katas that map to disciplines (**Flame Kata ≈ boxing**, **Adamantine Kata ≈ Gōjū-ryū**) (B). Sandrovich openly prioritizes entertainment over strict realism.

**Realism:** **6/10** — correctly named/differentiated styles, superhuman "Removal" feats.

---

## Naruto — Gentle Fist & Drunken Fist

The Hyūga **Gentle Fist (Jūken)** strongly resembles **Baguazhang** — the very name family "**Eight Trigrams** Sixty-Four Palms" mirrors Bagua's eight-trigram (*bagua*) nomenclature, and the circular vital-point palm strikes match (B for the resemblance). **Caveat:** no Kishimoto interview confirms Baguazhang as the source, so "intended as Bagua" is **(C)** fan attribution. **Rock Lee's Drunken Fist (Suiken)** explicitly evokes real **Zui Quan** (B).

**Realism:** **4/10** — real-art *inspiration* layered onto a fantasy chakra system.
