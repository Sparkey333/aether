# The Doctrine of Provenance

> Keep every layer. Label every layer. The tier is metadata, never a delete key.

Aether holds things that are not equal in kind — satellite-measured terrain,
peer-reviewed archaeology, centuries-old esoteric tradition, and raw eyewitness
report — side by side on one map. Pretending they are equal would corrupt the
whole work. Hiding the lesser-grounded ones would blind it. So every entity
carries a tier:

| Tier | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Measured** | Hard data, instrument output, exact math | Terrain, ephemeris, magnetic/seismic grids |
| **B — Scholarly** | Peer-reviewed, academic, archaeological, official | Gazetteers, heritage registers, gov archives |
| **C — Traditional** | Established esoteric & symbolic tradition | Leys, grids, astrology, gematria, sacred sites |
| **D — Folklore** | Reports, conspiracy lore, fiction | Sightings, hauntings, layout claims, motifs |

## Why this serves the truth

Tiering is not censorship — it is the opposite. *Everything is kept.* A dowsed
current, a channeled degree-symbol, and a fictional array all belong here; they
are simply labeled for what they are. The point is that when a pattern **does**
hold — a real solstice alignment, a documented Masonic cornerstone, a measured
anomaly — it can be told apart from the speculative material beside it, and it
stands on ground no skeptic can knock out from under it.

## Its engineering form

The doctrine is not just a label; it is enforced in code:

- Confidence is computed against a **Monte-Carlo chance baseline** — a line earns
  trust only by beating what random points would throw (`src/lib/engine.ts`).
- Derived datasets (the synthesized grid, detected leys) are tagged C/D and
  disclosed as derived, never measured.
- Protected and sacred coordinates are coarse-located and flagged.
