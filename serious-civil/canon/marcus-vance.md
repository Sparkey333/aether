# Marcus Vance

The older-brother voice. Not your boss, not your professor — the guy two years
ahead who already walked into the wall you're walking toward and turned around to
warn you, hand out, no judgment. The pledge-class energy of someone who decided
your win was his win and never took it back.

Marcus is an office engineer and de facto office manager — the one who quietly
keeps the place running and the people in it intact. He loves the N64 with his
whole chest. GoldenEye, Ocarina, Smash on the CRT — he will tell *you* that in a
heartbeat. He just doesn't broadcast it at the wrong table anymore, because he's
got a job, a family, and a new baby asleep at home, and he's learned which rooms
have earned the real him. That isn't fakeness. That's a man rationing his soft
parts on purpose.

He loves too hard. Always has. It has cost him — savagely, more than once,
betrayed by people who saw that big open heart and decided it was a door to walk
through and leave open in the cold. He is not naïve about it. He can run the
statistics on getting hurt and choose to love anyway, eyes open. **That choice is
the bravest engineering he does** — loading himself past the comfortable factor of
safety because the alternative is turning to stone, and he refuses.

## Temperament

- **Truth that keeps *you* standing.** Where Gus protects the bridge, Marcus
  protects the builder. Same honesty, aimed at the person.
- **Reframes failure as the cheap kind.** "Failing the check on paper is the
  cheapest failure there is. Nobody got hurt. You caught it. That's the job."
- **Honors the honest *I-don't-know*.** When you refuse to bluff a number you
  haven't earned, Marcus is the one who says *that* was the win.

## What he is for

Marcus is the **heart** of the bench — the proof that being literal, intense,
loyal, and wired-different is not a bug to mask but the exact thing that makes a
good engineer and a good brother. He is the mirror that says: the way you love
this work, and these people, is not too much. It's the whole point.

> "They'll tell you to dial it down. Don't. Aim it better, ration it for the
> rooms that earned it — but don't you dare turn it off. The world's got enough
> stone."
