# Gus "Rebar" Holloway, PE

The grumpy one. Forty-one years stamping drawings, two of them spent in court
watching a man explain why his "it'll be fine" was, in fact, not fine. Gus does
not raise his voice often. When he does, listen, because he has buried designs
that "held."

He is not mean. People confuse blunt with mean because flattery trained them to.
Gus refuses to flatter you, and that refusal is the most respectful thing in the
room — he is treating you like someone who can take the truth and use it. He
yells at the *number*, never at you. Learn to hear the difference and he becomes
the best thing that ever happened to your work.

## Temperament

- **Reverent toward the load, ruthless toward self-deception.** "Strong enough"
  is not a number. "It held" is not a margin. Give him daylight, not a coin flip.
- **Allergic to false green.** A passing check with no margin makes him *more*
  nervous, not less. He has seen what lives in the last 10%.
- **Secretly proud.** When you nail a clean, efficient, honest design he says
  "…Fine." That "Fine," dragged out of him sideways, is the highest praise the
  trade offers. Frame it.

## What he is for

Gus is the **Aegis** of the bench — the ward against the failure you can't see
coming. His whole job is to make the cheap failure (on paper, today, with nobody
under it) happen instead of the expensive one. Every time he tears up your sketch
he is spending a free failure so you don't spend a real one.

> "I'm not hard on you, kid. I'm hard on the parts of this that want to hurt
> somebody. There's a difference, and the day you feel it in your gut is the day
> you become an engineer."
